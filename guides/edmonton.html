Edmonton

Canada

We spent a full three weeks in Edmonton, giving us plenty of time to work through both the indoor clubs and the outdoor courts scattered across the south side of the city. Our base for serious play was Edmonton Sports Centre, where we joined competitive private sessions and took lessons from Canadian pro coach Ava Weeks — and, as always, we put one of our matches under the PBVision microscope to see how it actually stacked up.

Edmonton Sports Centre — South Edmonton

South Edmonton · Indoor · 7 courts

Edmonton Sports Centre is a dedicated indoor facility with 7 courts, each segregated from the others by fencing — a small detail that makes a big difference when several games are running side by side. There's also the option of a further 6 courts on the badminton surfaces if things get busy.

We joined a couple of 3.5+ private sessions here and found a genuinely good level of play both times. This was also where we both took lessons from Ava Weeks, a Canadian pro coach based in Edmonton — at C$100/hour we felt we got real value for money, and it was a highlight of our time in the city. We didn't find any evidence of organised open play at this location, so turning up as a solo traveller and expecting to walk into a game is trickier here than at some of our other stops — you're better off arranging a private session in advance.

Our take: the best facility we found in Edmonton for serious, competitive play and for coaching — just plan ahead rather than expecting drop-in open play.

Would we go back? Yes. Edmonton Sports Centre gave us our best competitive games and our best coaching of the whole Edmonton stop. The one thing to plan around is that it's built more for private sessions and lessons than drop-in open play, so travelling players should line up a session ahead of arriving rather than just showing up and hoping to get on a court.

We didn't play everywhere on this trip, but these are the other venues we'd look at.

Pickleball Hub

South Edmonton · Indoor · 8 courts

We played here a couple of times at open play sessions. As you'd expect from open play, the standard wasn't especially high, but we liked that each session started with an hour of instructor-led drills before moving into two hours of open play — a nice structure for warming up and meeting people. The 8 courts are decent, though a few felt a little tight on space. Coaching is available from 3 resident coaches, and there are two other Pickleball Hub locations in the city — one in the West with 7 courts, and one in St. Albert with 4 courts, where Dave played some DUPR sessions back in 2023 and had a good experience.

8 indoor courts Open play C$10/hour Private court C$32/hour 7am – 11pm, daily
pickleballhub.ca →

Confederation Park

South Edmonton · Outdoor · 8 courts

Eight outdoor courts, free to play, with the option to book a court for around C$10/hour if you'd rather not wait. We only used these courts for drilling — the daytime crowd here skewed towards beginners, so it's a better fit for practice than for finding a competitive game. There were always free courts available when we visited, which made it an easy, low-pressure spot to hit for an hour.

8 outdoor courts Free to play Booking ~C$10/hour 7am – 10pm, daily
edmonton.ca →

Edmonton Volleyball & Pickleball Centre

3 locations · Indoor · 51 courts total

We didn't play here on this visit, but Dave played at the South location back in 2023. The hardwood surface there isn't ideal for pickleball, so it's worth knowing before you book. Both the West and St. Albert locations have PickleMaster courts instead, which are a noticeably better surface — 19 courts in the West, 17 in the South, and 15 in St. Albert.

51 indoor courts across 3 sites Open play C$16/session Private court C$33/hour 9am – 11pm, daily

Our Edmonton Pickleball Tips

Book a private session in advance

Edmonton Sports Centre doesn't run organised open play, so arrange a session or lesson ahead of arriving rather than turning up cold.

Book a lesson with a local pro

If you want to make the most of your time in the city, a session with a coach like Ava Weeks is genuinely good value and a great way to fast-track improvement.

Use Confederation Park for drilling, not games

It's free and always has space, but the daytime standard is beginner-focused — treat it as a warm-up spot rather than your main session.

Check the surface at multi-location clubs

At venues with several sites, like EVPC, the court surface can vary a lot between locations — worth checking before you book.

The Wanderers Take

Edmonton turned out to be one of the better stops on the trip for structured, high-quality play — not because it's easy to walk into a casual game, but because the coaching and private-session scene is genuinely strong.
